00 / Statement Artist Statement

A curve drawn by a joint in motion cannot repeat — it can only return, shifted.

I am an Iranian-born artist working at the intersection of classical textile logic and generative systems. My work begins with a simple observation: the woven surface — repetitive, mathematically structured, infinitely variable — has always been a form of computation, long before computers.

Spirograph takes the mechanical toy and rebuilds it in code. Two arms are linked at a joint — the first arm rotates around the centre, the second rotates around the joint. The tip traces a curve that is neither random nor drawn: it is the unavoidable product of two sine waves with different speeds.

Each seed picks a speed pair, a set of arm lengths, an initial phase, and a two-tone colour palette. The composition is pre-computed for one full rotation of the inner arm, then drawn as a gradient-stroked path with a black shadow behind it — a single continuous line that drifts, folds, and returns.

On AI tools — where I encountered technical obstacles, I turned to AI tools for debugging and code optimization. Every structural, aesthetic, and conceptual decision remained my own.

01 / Seed

Variation

A numeric seed initializes the two background tones, the foreground colour, the pair of rotation speeds, the arm lengths, and the initial phases. Each output is regenerated and documented as a distinct, traceable work.

02 / Linking

Geometry

Two arms are joined at a pivot. The first rotates around the centre at speed 1; the second rotates around the joint at speed 2. The tip traces one continuous curve, pre-computed for one full rotation, then drawn with a gradient stroke.

03 / Surface

Application

The resulting composition becomes a surface-design asset — explored across garments, textile prints, wallpaper and other pattern-driven applications.
